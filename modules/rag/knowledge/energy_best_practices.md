# Campus Energy Efficiency Best Practices

## Overview

Reducing energy consumption in campus buildings requires a combination of
operational changes (low/no cost), equipment upgrades (moderate cost), and
infrastructure improvements (capital investment). This document focuses on
measures applicable to Indian educational institutions.

---

## 1. Lighting

### Quick Wins (No Cost)
- Switch off lights in unoccupied rooms, corridors, and toilets.
- Use natural daylight in labs and classrooms — keep blinds open during daytime.
- Implement a "last person out" checklist for every room.

### Low-Cost Measures
- Replace remaining fluorescent tubes with LED equivalents (70% energy saving).
- Install occupancy sensors in toilets, storerooms, and infrequently used corridors.
- Use LED strip lighting for notice boards and signage instead of fluorescent backlighting.

### Impact
- Lighting accounts for 20–35% of electricity in academic buildings.
- Full LED conversion of a 3,000 m² building typically saves 15,000–25,000 kWh/year.

---

## 2. HVAC (Air Conditioning and Ventilation)

### Operational Changes
- Set AC thermostat to 24°C (BEE recommended minimum for comfort; 18°C wastes ~6x more energy).
- Turn off AC 15 minutes before end of class; residual cooling is sufficient.
- Do not cool empty rooms — install door sensors or schedule-based auto-off.
- Clean air filters monthly; clogged filters increase energy consumption by 10–15%.

### Scheduling
- Use timer switches to prevent AC operation outside campus hours (7am–9pm).
- During semester breaks, operate AC only in data centres and server rooms.
- Raise setpoints by 2°C on weekends and holidays.

### Equipment Upgrades
- Upgrade to 5-star BEE-rated inverter ACs when replacing units.
- A 5-star inverter AC uses 40–50% less energy than a 2-star fixed-speed unit.
- For large halls, install variable frequency drives (VFDs) on AHU fans.

### Impact
- HVAC is typically the largest single energy end-use in Indian campuses: 40–60%.
- A 2°C setpoint increase from 22°C to 24°C saves approximately 10% on AC energy.
- Scheduling alone (turning off AC after hours) can save 20–30% of HVAC consumption.

---

## 3. Computers, Labs, and Equipment

### Operational
- Enable power management (sleep after 10 minutes, hibernate after 30 minutes) on all PCs.
- Turn off computers and monitors at the end of every working day — do not leave on standby.
- Shut down servers not in use overnight; virtualise workloads to consolidate servers.

### Lab Equipment
- Fume hoods are major energy consumers — keep sash closed when not in use.
- Turn off analytical instruments (centrifuges, ovens, spectrometers) when not running experiments.
- Ultra-low temperature freezers: consolidate samples, defrost regularly, keep away from heat sources.

### Impact
- IT and lab equipment can represent 15–25% of a campus's electricity use.
- Enabling PC power management across 500 computers saves approximately 50,000 kWh/year.

---

## 4. Water Heating

- Install solar water heaters for hostel blocks (typically 4–6 year payback in India).
- Insulate hot water pipes to reduce thermal losses.
- Fix leaking hot taps immediately — a dripping tap wastes 3,000–5,000 litres/year of hot water.

---

## 5. Building Envelope and Passive Design

- White or light-coloured roof paint reduces cooling load by 10–20% in Indian climate.
- Install external shading (chajja, louvers) on west-facing windows.
- Roof gardens reduce peak summer temperatures in top-floor rooms by 2–4°C.
- Seal gaps around doors and windows to reduce infiltration losses.

---

## 6. Energy Monitoring and Metering

- Install sub-meters at building level (not just campus-level meter).
- Display real-time energy dashboards in common areas — awareness reduces consumption by 5–10%.
- Conduct monthly energy audits: compare actual vs expected consumption.
- Use anomaly detection to flag buildings consuming >2 standard deviations above their mean.

---

## 7. Renewable Energy

### Rooftop Solar PV
- Indian campuses receive 4.5–6 kWh/m²/day of solar irradiance.
- A 100 kWp rooftop system generates approximately 130,000–145,000 kWh/year.
- Payback period: 4–6 years with current capital costs (~Rs 45,000–55,000 per kWp).
- Net metering allows export of surplus generation back to the grid.

### Solar Water Heating
- Flat-plate collectors: Rs 8,000–15,000 per 100 litre/day system.
- Suitable for hostels, canteens, and guest houses.

---

## 8. Cafeteria and Catering

- Use induction cooking instead of LPG where possible (cleaner, safer, comparable cost).
- Insulate cooking vessels; use pressure cookers to reduce cooking time and energy.
- Refrigeration: ensure door seals are intact; avoid opening cold rooms unnecessarily.

---

## 9. Behavioural Energy Efficiency Programme

- Run a semester-long "Green Campus Challenge" between hostels/departments.
- Display monthly leaderboard showing kWh/occupant for each building.
- Award certificates to the most improved building each semester.
- Include energy efficiency in induction programmes for new students and staff.
